5 Steps for Preventing and Responding to Falls in Roofing

June 11, 2023 · 3 min read · Travis S. McConnell

As a roofing contractor, protecting your workers is a top priority. One of the most significant risks in roofing is the potential for falls, which can lead to severe injuries and fatalities. Implementing preventive measures not only protects your workers but also helps safeguard your business from legal and financial consequences. This post discusses five essential steps that roofing contractors can take to prevent and respond to falls.

  1. Comprehensive Training and Education:

Proper training is key to preventing falls. Ensure that all workers receive comprehensive safety training specific to roofing tasks. This should include initial and periodic training on fall protection systems, safe ladder use, and proper techniques for working on sloped surfaces. Regularly update these programs to keep up with the latest safety regulations and industry best practices. Be sure to document all of the training, including who was trained, and when and where it occured.

  1. Effective Fall Protection Systems:

Implementing effective fall protection is crucial for worker safety. Conduct a thorough review of each worksite to determine the most suitable fall protection measures. This may include guardrails, safety nets, personal fall arrest systems (PFAS), or a combination of these methods. Regularly inspect and maintain all fall protection equipment to ensure its functional and being used. Document these inspections, and maintain records of inspections for as long as possible.

  1. Enforce Proper Equipment Usage:

Providing workers with fall protection is the first step, but ensuring they use it is equally important. Regularly inspect and maintain all safety equipment. Train workers on the correct usage and inspection procedures for this equipment. Reinforce the importance of using fall protection by penalizing workers who do not use fall protection properly, and by incentivizing workers who comply with safety procedures.

  1. Ongoing Safety Communication and Supervision:

Establish clear communication regarding safety methods and expectations. Regularly communicate with workers about potential hazards, safe work practices, and any updates to safety policies. Encourage an open dialogue where workers can report concerns or suggest improvements. Effective communication and supervision creates a culture within your organization which will minimizing the chances of accidents and falls.

  1. Legal Protection and Documentation:

While prevention is crucial, it's also vital to have legal protection measures in place should a fall occur. To protect your business, consider the following steps:

a. Adequate Insurance Coverage: Maintain comprehensive liability insurance coverage that includes workers' compensation. Review and update your policy regularly to ensure it adequately protects your business.

b. Document Training and Enforcement Practices: Ensure that all safety training, supervision, and inspection activities are documented in writing. This type of documentation will be crucial in showing OSHA that the fall was not preventable and did not result from employer negligence.

c. Incident Reporting: Establish an incident reporting process. In the event of a fall, document the details thoroughly, including date, time, location, and any contributing factors. Make sure properly trained managers visit the site quickly to respond to the incident.

d. Legal Consultation: Consult with legal professionals experienced in construction safety and OSHA's regulations to seek guidance on how to respond to the incident and OSHA's investigation.

Preventing worker falls in the roofing industry requires a proactive approach to safety. While not all falls can be prevented, ensuring that your company prepares will help reduce worker injuries and help the company respond to incidents when they do occur.
