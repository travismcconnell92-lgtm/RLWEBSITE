The 5 Powers of The Florida Construction Lien

December 11, 2023 · 3 min read · Travis S. McConnell

In the state of Florida, contractors wield a powerful tool that significantly enhances their chances of receiving payment—the construction lien. This legal instrument serves as both a sword and a shield for contractors, providing a robust mechanism to secure payment from a customer who is delinquent on their bill. In this blog post, we will discuss five key reasons why construction liens are such an effective tool for getting paid:

1. The Lien Strikes Fear with the Risk of Foreclosure

Perhaps the most potent aspect of a construction lien is its ability to strike fear into the owner of the property. By filing a lien against a property, a contractor effectively opens the door to the possibility of foreclosure. The mere mention of foreclosure can be a powerful motivator for property owners to settle outstanding debts, as the potential loss of their property looms large. This fear factor often compels owners to prioritize resolving payment disputes promptly to avoid the serious consequences associated with foreclosure.

2. Lien as a Potential Violation of the Owner's Mortgage

Filing a construction lien introduces an additional layer of risk for property owners, as it may potentially violate the terms of their mortgage note. Many mortgage agreements include clauses that prohibit the property owner from encumbering the property with liens without the lender's consent. Violating these terms could trigger a default under the mortgage, putting the owner in a precarious financial situation. This added pressure incentivizes owners to address payment issues swiftly to avoid triggering mortgage defaults.

3. Clouding the Title: A Barrier to Selling the Property

A construction lien has the power to cloud the title of the property, creating a significant obstacle for property owners attempting to sell. Prospective buyers are often hesitant to purchase a property with a clouded title, as it poses potential legal complications and uncertainties. The cloud on the title effectively puts the ball in the property owner's court, compelling them to resolve the payment dispute and clear the title before proceeding with any sale.

4. Potential Recovery of Attorneys' Fees

In Florida, the construction lien statute allows the prevailing party to recover their attorneys' fees in litigation, which can add up significantly. This provision adds an extra layer of risk for the delinquent party, as they may be responsible not only for the overdue payment but also for the legal costs incurred by the contractor in the pursuit of their compensation. The prospect of facing a legal battle with the potential for substantial attorneys' fees often pushes parties to seek an amicable resolution.

5. Foreclosure as a Last Resort

The ultimate power of a construction lien lies in its potential for foreclosure. If all else fails and the payment dispute remains unresolved, the contractor can take legal action to foreclose on the property. This means that even if the property owner is otherwise insolvent or uncooperative, the contractor has a pathway to recover their payment through the sale of the property at auction.

In sum, the construction lien serves as a multifaceted tool for contractors in Florida, offering a combination of legal leverage and financial consequences to secure timely payment for their services. By understanding and strategically employing the power of construction liens, contractors can navigate payment disputes with confidence, knowing they have a robust mechanism in place to protect their interests.
