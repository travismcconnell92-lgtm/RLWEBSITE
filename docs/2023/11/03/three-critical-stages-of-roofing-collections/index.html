Three Critical Stages of Roofing Collections

November 3, 2023 · 2 min read · Travis S. McConnell

The residential roofing industry often faces the challenge of unpaid bills. These outstanding payments can disrupt your cash flow and hinder business operations. This blog post provides a structured approach to collecting unpaid bills, outlining the three critical phases of collection in residential roofing.

  1. In-House Collection Efforts: Start by initiating collection efforts within your company. Here are essential steps to consider:
    • Maintain Clear Communication: Keep the lines of communication open with your customers throughout the project. Regularly remind them of payment deadlines and provide flexible payment options.
    • Collect a Deposit: Consider requesting a substantial deposit before commencing the roofing project. This upfront payment secures your revenue and demonstrates the customer's commitment.
    • Timely Invoicing: Ensure you provide accurate and detailed invoices promptly upon completing the roofing work. Include a breakdown of services, materials, and any agreed-upon costs.
    • Payment Plans: Offer payment plans or financing options in cases where customers may need assistance meeting their financial obligations.
  2. Attorney Involvement and Demand Letters: If in-house efforts fail to produce results, it may be time to involve an attorney specializing in collections. They can send a formal demand letter, often motivating the customer to take action. Here's how to make this stage more effective:
    • Hire an Attorney: Seek legal counsel experienced in debt collection specific to the construction industry. They can assess the situation and recommend the appropriate legal steps.
    • Demand Letter: The attorney will send a formal demand letter detailing the debt, payment terms, and potential consequences of non-payment. This formal notice can spur the customer to resolve the issue.
    • Negotiation: Encourage your attorney to explore negotiation and settlement options with the customer to avoid moving forward with litigation. A fair resolution benefits both parties and can save time and resources.
  3. Instituting Litigation: When all other options have been exhausted, and the customer remains uncooperative, the last resort may be to initiate litigation. This step is complex and costly, but sometimes it's necessary. Here's how to handle it effectively:
    • Consult Your Attorney: Work closely with your attorney to assess the viability of litigation, considering the amount owed and the likelihood of success.
    • Prepare Your Case: Collaborate with your attorney to gather all relevant documents, such as contracts, invoices, and communications. This evidence will be crucial in court.
    • Commitment: Understand that litigation can be a lengthy and expensive process. Be prepared to follow through with the case until a judgment is obtained.

Closing

Unpaid bills are a common challenge in the residential roofing industry. However, by following a structured approach to collections and heeding the recommendations provided, roofing companies can increase the likelihood of receiving overdue payments.
