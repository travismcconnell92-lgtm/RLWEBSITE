Addressing Title Issues as a Florida Homeowner

November 25, 2023 · 3 min read · Travis S. McConnell

Buying a home is a significant milestone, often accompanied by a myriad of paperwork and legalities. Amidst the pile of documents, one that stands out but is often overlooked is the title insurance policy. Title insurance is a crucial aspect of real estate transactions, offering protection against potential issues that may arise with the property's title. However, there are times when unforeseen problems do emerge, leading homeowners to initiate a title insurance claim.

Understanding Title Insurance

Before delving into the claims process, let's grasp the concept of title insurance. Unlike traditional insurance policies that protect against future events, title insurance focuses on the past. It safeguards homeowners and lenders against any hidden defects in the property's title that may surface after the purchase.

A title insurance claim is a formal request made to the title insurance company, seeking financial assistance to rectify issues with the property's title. Common reasons for filing a claim include wild deeds, boundary disputes, undisclosed heirs, and forgery, among others.

Identifying Your Title Insurer

The first step in initiating a title insurance claim is identifying who your title insurer is. This information can typically be found in your title insurance commitment, a document provided during the closing of your home purchase. If you no longer have this document, reaching out to the title company that facilitated the closing can help you determine the insurer.

Common Reasons for Title Insurance Claims

Title insurance claims can arise from a variety of issues, each posing a potential threat to your property rights. Wild deeds, for instance, refer to unauthorized or forged deeds that may cloud the title's legitimacy. Boundary disputes often occur when the property lines are unclear or disputed by neighboring landowners. Other common issues include outstanding liens, encroachments, and errors in public records.

Options for Handling a Title Insurance Claim

Once you've identified your title insurer, the next step is deciding how to proceed with the claim. Two primary options exist – hiring an attorney or drafting the claim yourself.

Seeking legal assistance is often the preferred route, especially when dealing with complex title issues. An attorney with expertise in real estate law can navigate the intricacies of the claims process, ensuring that all legal requirements are met. While this approach provides a level of assurance, it comes with associated legal fees, potentially adding to the overall cost of resolving the title issue.

For those comfortable navigating legal processes, drafting the title insurance claim independently is an option. This approach can save on legal fees but requires a thorough understanding of the title insurance policy and the specific claim requirements. It's crucial to follow the notice provisions outlined in the policy meticulously to avoid any potential setbacks.

Initiating the Title Insurance Claim

Regardless of the chosen approach, instituting a title insurance claim involves putting the title insurance company on notice. This is typically done by sending a formal written notice outlining the issue, the basis for the claim, and the requested resolution. Adhering to the notice provisions specified in the title insurance policy is crucial for a successful claim.

Conclusion

Initiating a title insurance claim is a process that demands attention to detail and a thorough understanding of the intricacies involved. Whether you choose to enlist the help of a legal professional or embark on drafting the claim independently, ensuring compliance with the title insurance policy's notice provisions is paramount. As a homeowner, being proactive in addressing title issues with the support of your title insurance can safeguard your property rights and provide peace of mind in your homeownership journey.
