Construction Delays: Disputes and Resolutions

November 2, 2023 · 3 min read · Travis S. McConnell

Imagine a bustling construction site, with architects, engineers, contractors, and subcontractors working diligently to bring a project to fruition. In an ideal world, every construction project would proceed seamlessly, adhering to schedules and budgets. However, in the real world, schedule delays are not uncommon and can disrupt the best-laid plans.

Determining Responsibility

One of the critical aspects of dealing with schedule delays is determining who is responsible for them. In many cases, responsibility is defined by the contract, specifying the party liable for delays. If the contract is silent on the matter, responsibility may be assessed based on other factors, such as the parties' dealings with each other and common industry practices.

In the case of an example, "Project X," the contract clearly designates the general contractor as responsible for adhering to the agreed-upon schedule. However, due to unforeseen weather conditions, the project faces delays that neither party could have anticipated. In such instances, assessing responsibility becomes a more intricate process, influenced by the specifics of the situation.

Informal Resolution before Formal Legal Disputes

Constructive collaboration and maintaining a positive working relationship among the project stakeholders are crucial when dealing with schedule delays. To resolve conflicts related to schedule delays, parties should aim to settle them informally through negotiation and discussion before resorting to formal dispute resolution or litigation.

In Project X, both the owner and general contractor understand the challenges caused by the unforeseen weather conditions. Instead of rushing into legal action, they decide to hold a meeting to discuss the issues openly. This informal resolution approach enables them to exchange information, understand each other's concerns, and explore potential solutions. By doing so, they maintain a more positive working relationship, which can be critical for the project's success.

The Importance of Documentation and Notice

While informal resolution methods are essential, it is equally crucial for each party to document their position and the circumstances surrounding schedule delays. Detailed records help in maintaining transparency and provide a solid foundation in case formal dispute resolution becomes necessary.

In the case of Project X, both the owner and general contractor diligently record all the weather-related delays, the impact on the project schedule, and the additional costs incurred. These records become valuable evidence in case they need to prove their positions in a dispute or refer to them for negotiation.

In addition to documenting the delays, the parties set out their position to one another in timely written notices, sent by email and certified mail. This not only builds a "paper trail" for later disputes, but may be required by the contract in order to avoid waiving a claim.

Common Contract Clauses Dealing with Schedule Delays

When experiencing project delays, it is crucial for the parties to review their written contract with one another to determine if the contract contains any clauses addressing responsibility, required notices, and resolution procedures. Many construction contracts include clauses addressing schedule delays, offering a structured approach to handling such situations. Two notable contract clauses are:

  1. Liquidated Damages Clause: This clause outlines predetermined daily or weekly monetary penalties that the contractor must pay if they fail to meet project deadlines. In Project X, the contract contains a liquidated damages clause, specifying a daily penalty for schedule delays.
  2. Consequential Damages Clause: This clause addresses the indirect or secondary costs that may result from schedule delays, such as additional rental expenses, lost profits, or increased financing costs. Project X includes a consequential damages clause, which allows the owner to claim these costs if the delays can be attributed to the contractor.

Closing

In the world of construction projects, schedule delays are almost inevitable. Determining responsibility for these delays is influenced by the contract and other relevant factors. Collaborative efforts should be made to resolve schedule delays informally before considering formal dispute resolution or litigation. However, diligent documentation of positions and circumstances is vital to support these processes. The presence of key contract clauses, such as liquidated damages and consequential damages clauses, can provide a structured framework for addressing schedule delays. By effectively managing these challenges, stakeholders can enhance the chances of successful project completion and maintain a positive working relationship.
